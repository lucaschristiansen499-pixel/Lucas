# Algebra — Compound Inequalities ("And" / Intersection)

## TL;DR
Solving "and" compound inequalities by finding the intersection (overlap) of two solution sets, reading/writing interval notation, and solving inequalities written in compact three-part form (e.g., 3 < 5 − x < 9).

## Terms
- **Compound inequality** — two inequalities joined by the word "and" or "or."
- **Intersection (∩, "upside-down U")** — the solution set of an "and" compound inequality: only the values that make **both** inequalities true at once.
- **Compact form** — writing an "and" compound inequality as one three-part inequality (e.g., 3 < 5 − x < 9) instead of two separate inequalities joined by "and." Only works for "and" statements, not "or."
- **Interval notation** — writing a solution set as a range. A square bracket `[ ]` means the endpoint is included; a parenthesis `( )` means it's not.
- **No solution / ∅ (empty set)** — what you write when the two inequalities in an "and" statement don't overlap at all. Can be written as the words "no solution," the symbol ∅, or `{ }`.

## Steps
**Solving two separate "and" inequalities:**
1. Graph each inequality on its own number line.
2. Find where the two graphs overlap — that overlap is the solution.
3. If the two ranges never overlap, the answer is "no solution."

**Solving a compact three-part inequality:**
1. If it's easier, split it into two separate inequalities joined by "and" and solve each normally.
2. To solve it directly: perform add/subtract steps first, doing the *exact same operation to all three parts at once*.
3. Then do any multiply/divide steps, again to all three parts.
4. If you multiply or divide all three parts by a **negative** number, flip both inequality signs.
5. Rewrite the inequality smallest-to-biggest before converting to interval notation.

## Worked examples
- **Sets warm-up:** A = odd numbers greater than 0 and less than 9 = {1, 3, 5, 7}. The intersection with set B keeps only the numbers that appear in *both* sets (e.g., 7 was confirmed to be in both).
- **Compact form, simple:** Solve **3 < 5 − x < 9**
  - Subtract 5 from all three parts: −2 < −x < 4
  - Multiply all three parts by −1 (flip both signs): 2 > x > −4
  - Rewrite smallest to biggest: −4 < x < 2
  - Interval notation: **(−4, 2)**
- **Compact form, more steps:** after subtracting 5, then multiplying all three parts by 3: −30 ≤ 2x ≤ −6
  - Divide all three parts by 2 (positive — no sign flip): −15 ≤ x ≤ −3
  - Interval notation: **[−15, −3]**
  - Shortcut mentioned: sometimes you can multiply through by the reciprocal in one combined step instead of doing multiply and divide as two separate steps.

## Watch out for
- An "and" compound inequality can have **no solution** if the two ranges never overlap (e.g., x > 4 and x < −2). This is different from "or" statements (tomorrow's topic), which keep both ranges no matter what.
- Multiplying or dividing by a **negative** number flips the inequality sign — this applies to all three parts in a compact inequality, not just one.
- Compact three-part form only works for "and" statements — you cannot write an "or" compound inequality in compact form.
