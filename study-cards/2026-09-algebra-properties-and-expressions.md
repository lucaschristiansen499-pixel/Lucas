# Algebra — Real Number Properties & Writing Expressions

## TL;DR
Homework review (identifying subsets, natural numbers vs. real numbers, integers) followed by a new lesson: translating word sentences into equations/inequalities, and the three key properties of real numbers (commutative, associative, distributive) used to simplify algebraic expressions.

## Terms
- **Subset** — a set fully contained inside another set. The smaller set goes on the "subset" side (e.g., natural numbers ⊂ real numbers — not the other way around).
- **Natural numbers** — your basic counting numbers (1, 2, 3, …).
- **Real numbers** — basically everything: whole numbers, fractions, decimals, negatives, etc.
- **Integers** — whole numbers and their negatives only (no fractions, no decimals).
- **Commutative property** — swapping the order doesn't change the result: a + b = b + a, and a × b = b × a. Only works for addition and multiplication.
- **Associative property** — moving the parentheses (regrouping) doesn't change the result: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c). Only works for addition and multiplication.
- **Distributive property** — a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the outside term across everything inside the parentheses.
- **Like terms** — terms with the same variable that can be combined by adding/subtracting their numbers.

## Steps
**Turning a word sentence into an equation or inequality:**
1. Find the "equals" word (is, equals, is the same as) or comparison word (less than, greater than) in the sentence.
2. Translate the math phrases in order: *product* = multiply, *difference* = subtract, *sum* = add.
3. Write it out with symbols. The multiplication dot is optional/implied in front of parentheses.
4. For inequalities: the point of the symbol always points to the smaller number.

**Simplifying an algebraic expression with parentheses:**
1. Check the sign in front of the parentheses.
2. If it's negative, rewrite it as "+ (−1)" and distribute that −1 to every single term inside — this flips every sign inside the parentheses. (This is the step to be most careful with.)
3. If it's positive, there's nothing to distribute — just drop the parentheses.
4. Use the commutative property to rearrange terms so variables are grouped together and plain numbers are grouped together.
5. Combine the like terms (add/subtract their coefficients).

## Worked examples
- **Homework check:** 5 × (−1/50) = (5/1) × (−1/50) = −5/50 = **−1/10** (either cross-reduce first, or multiply straight across then reduce — both work).
- **Subset (#46):** "Real numbers are a subset of natural numbers" → **False** — natural numbers are the smaller set inside real numbers, not the reverse.
- **Integers (#32):** from the given set — whole numbers count, a value that simplifies to a whole number (e.g., a root equal to 6) counts, but an irrational decimal does not.
- **Sentence → equation:**
  - "The product of negative four and x is twenty" → **−4x = 20**
  - "Three times the difference of v and three equals nine" → **3(v − 3) = 9**
  - "The sum of x and five is the same as three less than twice s" → **x + 5 = 2s − 3**
- **Sentence → inequality:**
  - "The difference of x and three is less than or equal to 5" → **x − 3 ≤ 5**
  - "Two is less than the sum of four and one-half z" → **2 < 4 + ½z**
- **Word problem → expression:**
  - Parking meter with x dimes → **0.10x** (value per coin × number of coins)
  - Two numbers add to 16, one is x → the other is **16 − x**
  - x is the first of two consecutive even integers → the next one is **x + 2** (consecutive even/odd integers are always 2 apart)
- **Simplifying:** group like terms (variables with variables, numbers with numbers), then combine — e.g., after distributing a negative through parentheses, combining −2 and −1 gives −3.

## Watch out for
- Commutative/associative properties work for addition and multiplication **only** — not subtraction or division (3 − 4 ≠ 4 − 3).
- Seeing parentheses doesn't automatically mean "associative" — check whether the *grouping* changed (associative) or just the *order* of the numbers (commutative).
- A negative sign directly in front of parentheses means distribute a −1 through **every** term inside — a common mistake spot.
